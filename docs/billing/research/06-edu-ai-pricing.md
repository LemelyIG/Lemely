# 06 - Education AI monetization benchmark (for Lemely, Egypt / EGP)

Researched 2026-11-02. Focus: HOW these products monetize (metering, gates, trials, refunds), prices are context.
Conventions: [V] vendor page, [3P] third-party/aggregator (unverified), "not found" = searched, no evidence. FX in this file is approximate (store trackers imply about EGP 50-56 per USD, Oct 2026). Raw per-product notes with every URL: `notes-a-student-tools.md`, `notes-b-teacher-tools.md`, `notes-c-uk-igcse.md`, `notes-d-patterns-egypt.md` in this folder.

## 0. Ten findings that matter

1. **No product that AI-marks past papers is a pure unlimited subscription.** Everyone meters (per question, per paper, per page, per submission, per day) and almost all hide a hard number behind a soft one. Unlimited is a marketing word on the top tier only.
2. **Two real per-paper price points exist.** Student side: AIDE AI sells pay-once page packs, $4.99 per 100 pages (12-month expiry), about $0.43-0.60 per full IGCSE paper [V learningaide.ai]. Teacher side: Grade Direct about $0.10 per "full exam marking" (about 30 credits, $25 buys 6,000 credits, no expiry) [V grade.direct]; DeepMark GBP 5 per 15 papers (33p) [V]. Marking.ai Starter/Pro/Plus work out to $0.32 / $0.20 / $0.18 per submission [V].
3. **Seasonal, non-renewing passes are the exam-market norm**: Tripos 4-month GBP 59, 6-month GBP 149; Up Learn "until exams" GBP 330-430; Seneca 4-month plan; Xtremepapers Prepare "Season Pass" $9/90 days with 7-day refund; examai.ie EUR 60 exam-year pass; Exam Ready Tools 3/6-month plans. Cambridge Oct/Nov and May/Jun peaks are the revenue calendar.
4. **Thea's cautionary tale**: it moved previously unlimited Smart Study/Practice Tests to daily limits plus a 1-2 hour cooldown, announced nothing in advance, and its 2026 reviews are full of "greedy" / "bait and switch". Users were angrier about the surprise and the thin free tier than about paying.
5. **Reviewers punish card-upfront auto-renew** more than any price: Save My Exams, Seneca, Mindgrasp, Turbo AI, Chegg (FTC $7.5M settlement 2025-09). The no-card, no-auto-renew products (Marking.ai, MasteryMind, Exaim, DeepMark, Thea itself) escape this complaint class entirely.
6. **Show-the-cost and refund-on-failure are rare but exist**: Marking.ai (failed submissions not charged, one-click retry), KEATH API (credits charged only after a validated result), Top Marks AI (shows credit total before class feedback runs), TeachEdge (credits shown after every action), Suno (auto-refunds on failed generation). Brainly Tutor is the counter-example (session counts against the 20/month even when it fails, [3P]). Being explicit here is a differentiator, not table stakes.
7. **Egypt is not priced like the US/UK**: Duolingo Super EGP 64.99/mo (-91% vs US), Gauth EGP 180, Quizlet Plus EGP 500, Brainly lowest IAP EGP 59.99, Photomath Plus EGP 78-350, but ChatGPT/Claude about EGP 1,000 (barely discounted) [3P store trackers, Oct 2026]. Human IGCSE tutoring EGP 350-1,200 per hour [3P vendor-published]. An AI mark at EGP 5-20 is a rounding error vs a tutor hour; a EGP 500/mo subscription is a real purchase.
8. **Payment reality** (CBE, Dec 2025): 4.8M credit cards, 21.6M prepaid, 19.7M debit, 60M registered wallets; youth (15-35) financial inclusion only 54%. Parents pay for minors; wallets, Fawry codes, InstaPay and prepaid scratch codes matter more than Visa.
9. **Free-tier numbers drift upward in annoyance over time** (CoGrader 100 -> 150, EssayGrader 50/100, Thea unlimited -> daily cap, Medly dropped its trial for a 3-questions/day freemium, Knowt 1 AI summary/month). Changing a published free allowance later costs trust; set it low-but-honest on day one.
10. **School/B2B is quote-only almost everywhere**, anchored at per-student-per-year: Khanmigo $15 list ($10 in a 87k-student deal), MagicSchool $3,999-9,999 per school or $6/student, Exaim $22-45/student/yr, examai.ai $3/student/month, KEATH about GBP 4/student, ReMarkAble from GBP 120/mo for 120 pupils, Writable about $1-9/student/mo (US data; MENA B2B not found).

## 2. Deep dives

### 2.1 Thea (thea.study / theastudy.com), full mechanics

**What it is.** US company (Theaai Corporation) AI study app: upload notes, PDFs, slides, images, YouTube links (90+ file types), get "study kits" (flashcards, study guides, summaries, games, Smart Study adaptive Socratic questions, Practice Tests), 80+ languages. Not an exam marker; no mark-scheme grading, no IGCSE/A-level marking. It does have an A-Levels hub that is just marketing. App Store 4.9 (about 25k reviews [3P apppricer]), Trustpilot 4.7 (54 reviews). Claims "over 90% of learners never pay" [V].

**Who pays.** Students (Thea+ individual); educators free ("Free for educators. Unlimited for your students." Students get full core access via the shared kit; no card, about 2 minutes setup); institutions quote-only "priced by student or by classroom", white-glove pilot, LMS integrations (Moodle, Blackboard, Brightspace, Google Classroom), FERPA/GDPR/HECVAT. Parents/tutors are targeted as "share a kit with your child". Terms: accounts are individual only, no sharing; minimum age 13 (parent review for minors). No parent-pays flow, no family plan, no student discount found.

**Free tier (the actual gate).** Unlimited study-kit creation, flashcards, study guides, games, uploads, and no account needed to try public kits. Smart Study and Practice Tests are metered with daily question limits and "wait times" (reviewers: "a couple questions then locked out for 2 hours", 2026-10 App Store; appy.fyi 2026-05 says "an hour-long wait"). Thea+ "removes those question limits and adds no wait times and premium features" (the premium features are not itemised publicly). Important design point: Thea meters the high-LLM-cost loop (generating fresh questions) and leaves the cheap, cacheable assets (cards, guides) free. That is the cost-control insight, not the price.

**Metering unit and enforcement.** Questions per day (generation of fresh Smart Study/test items), enforced as a server-side cooldown. Duration is described inconsistently (1h vs 2h), suggesting a tunable that Thea adjusts. No upload cap, no token cap, no credit wallet.

**Price and plans.** "Monthly and Annual plans"; price shown only inside My Profile after login ("prices vary"). Store prices [3P apppricer 2026-10-30; App Store]: US $12/mo, $48/yr; Canada $14/$57; Romania RON 22 (about $4.63) monthly (cheapest), Switzerland CHF 14 (about $17), Brazil R$59.90/mo and R$249.90/yr labelled "Early Access". Annual works out to about $4/mo (67% off monthly). Regional pricing is store-driven and varies about 4x. Egypt price not found. No free trial on any SKU (store listings show none), and no card is requested for the free product.

**Cancel / refund.** Desktop: My Profile -> Cancel; iOS/Android via store. Terms (updated 2025-03-20): cancel at least 1 day before term end; access continues to end of term then auto-downgrades; direct purchases are non-refundable except where law requires; no refund if price drops/promos; price changes announced in advance and applied at next renewal; "Cancellation by Us ... at any time for any reason".

**What users say.** Free tier "extremely generous" (Trustpilot GB, 2026-10-17); a reviewer bought nothing because "there is no such thing as premium" (2026-01) - so the paywall is new. After Thea+ launched: "greedy", "bait and switch", "I can't afford a subscription as a student", "if you need money put ads on it" (App Store, 2026-05 to 2026-10, 16 found one review helpful). Thea's reply: "Thea is still free ... limits designed to be modest". appy.fyi (2026-05) diagnosis: the anger is "entirely about monetization, not the product", biggest cluster is "previously-free core paywalled with no warning". Quality complaints that matter for a marking product: wrong math ("3x2=9", Trustpilot 2026-04), repetitive questions, uploads stuck, kit overload drops material.

**Lessons from Thea specifically.**
1. Meter generation, not storage. 2. A visible cooldown timer converts better than a hard wall but creates "greedy" resentment if introduced late. 3. Launch limits on day one; never retro-tighten a once-unlimited feature. 4. Free-for-educator is a distribution wedge (teacher shares kit -> students onboard free -> some convert), exactly Lemely's teacher-class path. 5. Pricing hidden behind login hurts SEO and trust, but lets them A/B regionally. 6. 90% non-payers is the explicit design: monetise a minority of heavy users; it only works because per-user free cost is tiny (cheap models, cached assets). Marking handwritten papers has a much higher free cost than flashcards, so Lemely cannot copy "90% free".

### 2.2 Marking.ai (closest teacher-side, credit mechanics)
- Unit: one submission = one student's work vs one assessment, marked for the first time. Re-mark, edit, regenerate feedback, summaries, exports, share links are free [V support.marking.ai 2026-09-01]. Handwriting from scan/photo PDF; one answer can span pages; mark scheme uploaded by teacher; every mark quotes student's own words; teacher approves before release (also contractual).
- Plans: allowance 90/250/500 per month, resets on billing date, no rollover. Top-up packs never expire and survive cancellation. Spend order: allowance -> top-up -> trial.
- Free: 20 submissions one time with school email (6 with Gmail), no card, full Pro features, earn more by referring teachers. At cap marking pauses; modal offers wait / upgrade / top-up; account and data stay.
- Failures: not charged; retry (bulk); plain-English failure type. Refund: 14-day on first payment (merchant of record Lemon Squeezy). USD billing with GBP display price creates an inconsistent public price (GBP 21 vs $29) - a trust paper cut.
- School: unlimited teacher seats, pooled annual allowance, admin sees usage not attainment, invoiced, no MAT product.
- Per-submission cost to the user: $0.32 / $0.20 / $0.18 depending on tier (a submission may be a multi-question paper).

### 2.3 Grade Direct (closest on per-paper economics and published accuracy)
- Credit meter with cost bands: basic ~3 credits (about 1 cent), in-depth 8-10 (about 3c), full exam about 30 (about 10c). $25 one-time = 6,000 credits, no expiry (about 200 full exams = $0.12 each). Free: 150 on signup, then 50/month. School: pooled credits, dedicated manager.
- No subscription = no cancellation or renewal friction; it is the cheapest product found that publishes accuracy on IGCSE (Mathematics 0580 papers 2 and 4: 561 questions, kappa 0.86, 84% exact, 97% within 1 mark, handwritten, 2026-07-17 vendor data).
- Calls OpenAI; "choose your AI model" lets teachers trade speed/cost/accuracy (cost-aware control surfaced to the user).
- Gap: no public cost preview per upload, no refund policy found, inconsistent copy ("under $10 per month" vs $25).

### 2.4 Save My Exams Smart Mark (the incumbent, student-side)
- Smart Mark is bundled, per question (not per paper), typed or handwritten, written questions only, selected STEM/Business/Eng Lit courses; initially no MCQ/calculation.
- Gate: unlimited only on Premium (about GBP 25/mo, GBP 96/yr); Essential (GBP 12/mo, GBP 48/yr) excludes it; free users get ONE taster question per course. 7-day trial on Premium annual only (card required, converts to annual).
- Cancel/refund: auto-renews; 14-day UK cooling-off if no premium content used; goodwill refunds for forgotten renewals within 14 days; double-click on Cancel ends access immediately; promo first term auto-renews at full price.
- Reviews: Trustpilot 4.6 (about 2,000) but recurring complaints about surprise renewals (GBP 50 annual after trial), Essential tier excluding MCQ, "ask to buy every time". Regional price "varies by account type, location", nothing published for Egypt. Account sharing grey market (Shopee resellers) exists.
- Lesson: the incumbent proves students will pay GBP 4-8/month-equivalent for AI marking on a curated question bank; its weakness is not uploads of arbitrary handwritten papers and a trial-to-annual trap.

### 2.5 AIDE AI + Tripos + ReMarkAble (the actual "mark my handwritten IGCSE paper" cohort)
- **AIDE AI** (Pakistan/Cambridge oriented, Arabic supported): pay-once page packs 12-month validity, 50 free pages no card; teacher $44/mo for 1,000 pages with top-ups $0.04/page and carryover if renewed within 5 days. Page unit is easy to explain and maps to cost (vision tokens). About $0.43-0.60 per paper.
- **Tripos**: 50 paper markings per calendar month (UTC reset, no rollover), weekly price GBP 5.99, 4-month GBP 59 and 6-month GBP 149 non-renewing passes, first paper free for 2 questions with no card. The seasonal pass is the interesting product.
- **ReMarkAble**: Edexcel International GCSE (34 subjects) and Cambridge IGCSE (51), 3 free assessments per 30 days (max 5 pages per upload), GBP 9.99/mo student; Teacher Class GBP 24.98/mo for 30 pupils; school from GBP 120/mo for 120 pupils (about GBP 1/pupil/mo). Page cap per upload is the guard against runaway cost.
- Regulatory context for the schools channel: Cambridge 2026 handbook says AI must not be sole or primary means of marking candidates' coursework; JCQ (Jan 2026) says evidence does not support AI marking for high-stakes qualifications. So vendors sell "practice/mock, teacher approves", never "official mark" (Tutopiya 2026-09-24 summary).

## 3. Cross-cutting patterns

**Metering units seen (most to least common in AI marking).** per submission (teacher tools) > per question/day (student freemium: Medly, MasteryMind, Smart Mark taster) > per paper/month (Tripos 50) > per page (AIDE, ReMarkAble 5/upload) > token-credits (TeachEdge 5k tokens, Grade Direct) > energy/hearts (Gizmo, Exaim, Duolingo, MathsGenie). Input-size caps (pages per doc, characters, words per essay) appear everywhere as a hidden second meter.

**Credits vs unlimited.** Consumer student apps sell "unlimited" tiers with soft caps (Quizlet Unlimited, Gizmo, Mindgrasp). AI-marking products meter. Industry data: hybrid (base + usage/credits) is dominant (Orb 2025: 92.4% of AI-agent companies hybrid; Poyar 2025: hybrid primary 27% to 41% in 12 months). Cursor's June 2025 change shows the risk: the unit change plus silent overage drew a public apology and refunds (TechCrunch 2025-07-07). Duolingo shows AI marginal cost collapses (video call under $0.01 per Q2 2026 call) so a premium AI tier can disappear - do not hardwire margins to today's token prices.

**Free-to-paid gate patterns.** (a) count-based at feature (Smart Mark 1 q/course, Marking.ai 20 once), (b) time-based cooldown (Thea, Gizmo, Brisk 30 min), (c) depth-based (Photomath), (d) content-contribution currency (Course Hero, Studocu, Knowt coins), (e) volume cap per month with visible countdown (Knowt).

**Trials.** Card-upfront trials auto-converting to annual are the single largest source of 1-star reviews (SME, Seneca, Mindgrasp, Turbo AI). Benchmarks: card-required trials convert about 30% vs about 5x lower without but cut signups (ChartMogul 2026-02, B2B SaaS); RevenueCat 2026: Education download-to-trial median 6.5%, trial-to-paid 34.2% North America vs 15.2% India+SE Asia, 5-9 day trials favored. No-card trials: Marking.ai, MasteryMind, Exaim (does not auto-convert), DeepMark, ReMarkAble, Tutopiya.

**Seasonal / term passes and guarantees.** Non-renewing session passes with a short refund window: Xtremepapers $9/90 days (7-day refund), Alt Academy exam pass (7-day refund), 10Exam $15/3 months, Tripos, Exam Ready Tools. Outcome guarantees as paywall closers: Up Learn (all payments refunded if 90% Up Score and miss A*/A; claim within 14 days of results), Seneca (+GBP 5.99/mo, refund if all courses completed and grade missed). Guarantee completion rates are low (Up Learn 2017: 60 of 180 completed [3P]) so guarantees are cheap to offer. Conversion data for guarantees: not found.

**Parent flows.** Apple Ask to Buy (request expires 24h) and Google Play approvals (do not cover non-prepaid subscriptions) make in-app kid purchase brittle; the standard bypass is parent buys on web and the child logs into the same account (Lingokids). Tutopiya "GuardianPay" (kid taps ask-a-parent, one-tap approve) is vendor-claimed only. Parent products found: Khanmigo ($44/yr parent view, up to 10 kids), Seneca parent account, Maths Genie free parent link plus gift cards, Atom Home (parent-pays GBP 39.99/mo), Exam Ready Tools (3 learners per account), MyEdSpace 10% sibling discount, Duolingo Family (annual only). Hard conversion data: not found.

**Refunds and failure.** Most consumer edu apps: "all sales final" (Turbo AI, Mindgrasp, Thea direct). Usage-conditional refunds: Course Hero (<=5 unlocks), Midjourney (<20 GPU minutes), Question.AI (weighs usage), Xtremepapers (not substantially used). Auto-refund on failure: Suno, Marking.ai, KEATH API.

**School/B2B.** Quote-only; per student per year; pooled annual allowance (Marking.ai, Top Marks); free for teacher to create demand then sell school (Gradescope, Brisk, Thea, Writable, Real Fast Reports "your school has subscribed"); pilots of 30 days to 6 months (Tutopiya, KEATH, Top Marks).

## 5. Lessons for Lemely (opinionated)

**1. Price per outcome the user understands: "marks per paper", not tokens.** Make the credit = one marked paper-equivalent, scaled by pages. Three transparent bands like Grade Direct (short quiz / structured paper / full paper) and weight by LLM cost internally (pages x question count), but display one number. A full IGCSE paper costs 10-60 cents of user price in the market; at Egypt purchasing power that is about EGP 5-30 per paper. Keep Lemely's cost target (<= $0.10/paper) as the floor margin check: Grade Direct sells at about $0.10-0.12 and AIDE at $0.43-0.60, so there is room for a premium on handwriting + mark-scheme fidelity.

**2. Show the cost before the AI action and refund automatically on failure. Make it a headline feature.** Only Marking.ai, KEATH, Top Marks and TeachEdge do any of this; no consumer edu product does. Pre-upload "this will use 3 credits (12 pages)" + "failed or unreadable scan = credits back automatically" directly answers the top review complaints (surprise limits, burned credits). Suno's policy is the template: refund on no output, timeout, safety-filter reject, server error; no refund for disliked-but-successful output. Re-mark/regenerate feedback = free (Marking.ai and EssayGrader both do this; it kills dispute tickets), but cap re-marks to prevent abuse and send genuine mark disputes to teacher review.

**3. Use hybrid: small monthly included allowance + top-up packs that do not expire.** Do not sell pure unlimited. Do not copy Thea's daily cooldown for marking (a 2-hour wait on a revision sprint is a churn trigger; reviewers hated it even at near-zero price). Recommended structure: signup grant (see 4) + monthly plan with credits that expire at period end (Suno/Midjourney/Marking.ai norm) + non-expiring top-up packs (Marking.ai, Grade Direct, AIDE 12-month expiry is a compromise). Spend order: plan credits -> top-up -> grant, as Marking.ai documents. Publish the order.

**4. Signup grant: small, honest, one-time, no card, shown on day one, and tied to the first paper.** Benchmarks: Marking.ai 20 submissions (6 on Gmail), Tripos first paper 2 questions, Maths Genie 1 paper ever, ReMarkAble 3 per 30 days, AIDE 50 pages, DeepMark 20 papers. Suggest grant = enough for exactly 1-2 full papers (the activation moment: student sees marked paper) not a recurring free allowance. Recurring free marking is not affordable at handwritten-vision cost (Medly's 3-questions/day freemium drew "extremely limiting" reviews; Thea can be generous only because flashcards are cheap). Use a referral top-up (Marking.ai, Real Fast Reports, Knowt coins) rather than raising the base grant. Show the school-email vs personal-email distinction only for teachers.

**5. Lead with seasonal exam-session passes, not auto-renew monthly.** Egyptian demand is calendar-bound (Cambridge Oct/Nov and May/Jun; Abwaab sells Term-1 and full-year bundles). Sell: pay-as-you-go credit packs (default), a non-renewing "Exam Season Pass" (about 4 months, bundled credits + extras) with a 7-day not-used refund (Xtremepapers/Alt Academy norm), and only then a monthly subscription for teachers/heavy users. Avoid card-on-file auto-renew traps (the number one review complaint across SME, Seneca, Mindgrasp, Chegg FTC). If you ever do auto-renew, email 7 days before, cancel in one click, and don't auto-convert trials.

**6. Make the parent the buyer for students under 18 and design for Egyptian rails.** Student requests credits -> parent link / WhatsApp message -> parent pays via Fawry code, Vodafone Cash, InstaPay, card, with web checkout (avoids Apple Ask to Buy; web fee about 2% vs 30% in-app, Duolingo's point). Offer prepaid scratch/activation codes sold through bookshops and tutoring centres (Egyptian platforms do), and a parent dashboard (Khanmigo, Seneca, Abwaab all show parents progress). Credit packs denominated in EGP (e.g. 50/100/200/500, mirroring Nagwa's EG Credit wallet) are familiar. Family: one parent wallet shared across children (Exam Ready Tools allows 3 learners/account; MyEdSpace sibling 10%).

**7. Regional pricing is mandatory and should be explicit.** Egypt is -70 to -90% vs US for consumer apps that bothered (Duolingo, Gauth, Quizlet), but only -1% to -11% for LLM subscriptions, which are bought by a narrower, richer segment. Lemely's anchors: Duolingo EGP 65, Gauth EGP 180, Quizlet EGP 500 (each a monthly cost); human tutor EGP 350-1,200/hour. A price of roughly EGP 100-250 for a bundle of papers, or EGP 150-400 for a season-pass tier, sits inside the demonstrated range; verify against actual cost per paper before fixing numbers. Charge in EGP via the local gateway (Marking.ai's GBP-display/USD-charge mismatch and Tutopiya SGD billing are both friction).

**8. Teachers: free class set + submission-based plans; schools pooled.** Copy Marking.ai structure: teacher allowance per month by submission (a submission = one student's paper marked once), re-mark free, teacher approves before release (also aligns with Cambridge/JCQ stance that AI cannot be sole marker; frame as "AI draft, teacher approves"). Teacher free tier like CoGrader (150/mo) is too generous for handwritten vision costs; use a one-time trial plus a small recurring allowance only for school-verified emails. School = pooled annual credits, invoiced, per-student-per-year anchor (Exaim $22-45, ReMarkAble about GBP 1/pupil/month, KEATH about GBP 4). Teacher-shared quizzes and graded class papers can "pull in" students the way Thea's free educator accounts do.

**9. Do not put quality-critical features behind the surprise.** Medly (marking inconsistency allegation, incentivised reviews [3P]) and Thea (math errors) show that a trust problem compounds a pricing problem. Publish accuracy numbers the way Grade Direct does (kappa, exact match, within +/-1 mark on real IGCSE questions) and show confidence/"review needed" flags; this justifies a premium over DeepMark/AIDE.

**10. Launch limits on day one and never retro-tighten.** Thea's unannounced shift from unlimited to capped drew the largest cluster of negative reviews; CoGrader/EssayGrader/Knowt show free numbers drifting. Publish the limit table, put a visible countdown ("credits reset in 12 days", Knowt) and show remaining balance after every action (TeachEdge). Announce any change 30+ days ahead and grandfather existing balances.

**11. Guarantees are cheap conversion levers.** Up Learn and Seneca attach conditional refunds to completion + outcome; the completion requirement means few claims. For Lemely a lightweight version is better: "first paper unsatisfactory (OCR unreadable / marks wildly off) -> credits back + human re-mark by a tutor from the marketplace". That also routes demand to the tutor marketplace and builds trust without a grade guarantee. Do not promise grades.

**12. Bundle the tutor marketplace as the high-end upsell, not another plan.** Pricing evidence: Up Learn Master includes 2 human-marked papers at GBP 105/mo; tutors cost EGP 350-1,200/hour. Offer "tutor review of this marked paper" as an add-on in credits (AI mark + 15-min tutor feedback), where the platform takes a commission. Use it as the upsell trigger when a student's repeated low scores appear.

**13. Keep two numbers separate in the data model.** (a) cost-weighted credits (internal, tied to LLM cost per paper) and (b) display price per credit by region. When AI costs drop (Duolingo video call under $0.01; Claude Haiku default since 2026-10-27) you can lower credits-per-paper silently (a better value) or keep margin; if costs rise you can raise credits-per-paper without changing the visible price. Keep a monthly job that compares realised cost per credit with price per credit and alert below target gross margin (industry AI gross margins 50-60% per Lazard; 29% of surveyed firms below 60%).

**14. Concrete starting offer (hypothesis to test, not a verified price list).**
- Signup grant: 1 full paper's worth of credits (about 30 credits), no card, one-time, expires in 30 days.
- Packs (non-expiring 12 months, EGP): small / medium / large with 15-25% volume discount, shown as "papers" (e.g. 3 / 10 / 25 papers).
- Season Pass (non-renewing, 4 months): fixed bundle of 12 papers + free re-marks + parent dashboard, EGP roughly the price of one tutor hour; 7-day not-used refund.
- Teacher plan: monthly submissions allowance with free re-mark, class analytics, school-email trial; school: pooled annual credits invoiced.
- Rules: preview cost before every action, automatic refund on technical failure, no card-on-file for packs/passes, EGP via Paymob wallets/Fawry/InstaPay/card, parent-buy flow for under-18s.
