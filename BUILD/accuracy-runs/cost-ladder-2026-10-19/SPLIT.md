## 12. What these numbers do and do not support

- binding_second_read is the largest tag in both runs (baseline 45.3%, A/A 60.7%); its thinking tokens alone are 37.7% / 50.8% of total spend. Correction is 20.4% / 27.7%, so correction-side levers (A1, A2, A3) have a lower ceiling than binder-side ones (B1, B2).
- The baseline extraction share (34.1% vs 11.4% in A/A) is one 65,522-token runaway call that did not recur in the A/A on the same prompt: B12 on extraction is priced off a single event, so its saving is an upper bound conditional on a runaway happening, not an expected value per script.
- Second-read output varies run to run for the same script (table in section 6), so B1/B2 per-script savings carry that variance as well as the assumed rho/trigger rate.
- The baseline is not a clean full-price reference: it ran with a read/write cache (one case, 10 calls, free) and contains one runaway extraction call. The A/A (cache bypass) is the clean $/script figure; the baseline mean is shown only for continuity with summary.json.
- A/A churn floor is 1 mismarked part out of 78 (one discordant leaf, McNemar p=1.00); with n discordant = 1 the floor is a single observation, so it is underpowered as an estimate of run-to-run noise. Any later delta of <= 1 mismarked part is inside it by rule.
- B1, B2, A2 and the A3 hypothetical rows depend on assumed rho, trigger rate, re-mark rate and prefix share. They are scenario arithmetic, not measurements. Only B12-extraction, A1 and the A3 as-is row are fully determined by the recorded tokens, and A1 ignores Claude/flash-lite tokenisation and quality.
- Nothing here supports calling any lever an improvement: no lever was run, the corpus is 12 scripts over 5 papers, and the gate is non-regression within the floor.
